Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, **"From Code Repository to Research
Infrastructure: Evaluating GitHub for Managing the Scientific Research Lifecycle,"** for
publication as a research article in *Scientometrics*.

Research projects generate many interdependent artifacts — a question, a protocol, a
literature corpus, data, analysis code, results, a manuscript, released software — that in
common practice are managed across disconnected tools, leaving the *process* that produced
the outputs poorly traceable. A large body of work addresses the stewardship of research
*outputs* (repositories, persistent identifiers, curation); far less addresses, on an
evidentiary basis, the management of the process itself. This manuscript asks how far one
widely available platform, GitHub, can close that gap.

The study combines methods that are central to this journal's scope:

- a **bibliometric analysis** of a 5,139-work OpenAlex and arXiv corpus (2008–2025),
  cross-checked against Scopus and Web of Science, characterising how the literature on
  digital infrastructure for research-process management has grown and which lifecycle
  stages it under-serves;
- a **structured requirement extraction** from 67 review, synthesis and primary studies,
  yielding fifteen lifecycle-structured research management requirements;
- a **reproducible coverage analysis** mapping GitHub's 68 native capabilities onto those
  requirements with an explicit four-level rubric; and
- a **design synthesis** — a reference architecture and a reusable template — tested in a
  self-referential implementation and re-instantiated on an unrelated published project.

The central finding is a convergence reached by two independent routes: the literature
under-serves idea and question formulation, planning and decision traceability, and GitHub
supports those same requirements markedly less well (mean 1.67 of 3, versus 2.50 for the
rest). GitHub emerges as a coordination and traceability *layer* for a substantial part of
the lifecycle — strong from execution to output, weak upstream, and dependent on external
infrastructure for preservation and large data — that *maps* the research-process-management
gap rather than closing it. All data, coding sheets, mapping tables and analysis scripts
are openly available and archived on Zenodo, and every table and figure is regenerated by a
versioned script.
